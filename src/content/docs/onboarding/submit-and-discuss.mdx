Submit only after the provided build, correctness test, and benchmark complete
in your working copy.

## Before you submit

- Confirm that only `src/submission.hpp` contains your changes.
- Confirm that the code is your own work.
- Remove temporary diagnostics, generated files, and local benchmark artifacts
  from the proposed change.
- Run the preset build and correctness test one final time.
- Review the change so you can explain every design decision.

## How to submit

Submissions are made as a **pull request from your own fork** — you do not need
any credentials or workflow changes.

1. **Fork** the
   [template repository](https://github.com/UWHPC/onboarding-template) to your
   own GitHub account.
2. Implement your solution in `src/submission.hpp` and commit it to a branch on
   your fork.
3. Open a **pull request** against `UWHPC/onboarding-template` with base branch
   `main`.

The evaluator runs automatically on your pull request and, within about a
minute, posts a **result comment** with your build and test results, benchmark
runtime, and score. When a submission fails, the comment also shows a short hint
naming the *category* of failure (for example, "non-square grids") — never the
specific hidden case. Push more commits to your branch to iterate; the comment
updates in place, and the pull request check turns green once your submission
passes.

Your pull request is automatically converted to a **draft**. This is expected —
you do not merge it. It stays open as your submission record for us to review.

The check passes only when the evaluator returns `completed`. The other terminal
states are `failed_build`, `failed_tests`, `failed_benchmark`, `timed_out`, and
`internal_error` — the first three point at your submission, the last two do
not. You do not configure evaluator credentials or modify the workflow, and you
should not alter the harness to bypass a failing or missing check.

Keep pushing commits and re-running until you are satisfied with your result;
there is no single required submission and the comment always reflects your
latest run. When you are done, join the UWHPC Discord from our
[contact page](https://www.uwhpc.com/contact) and make a post in the
**onboarding** forum channel with your name and a link to your pull request. We
will take it from there.

## Evaluation criteria

The review considers:

- Whether the implementation is correct
- The quality and clarity of the implementation
- The reasoning behind design decisions
- Performance on the team benchmark machine

Correctness is required but is not the only consideration. The problem
statement says applications are reviewed holistically and are not advanced on
benchmark score alone.

## Design discussion

After submission, candidates may be invited to a short virtual chat about their
work. Prepare to explain:

- How you interpreted the requirements
- The design choices you made
- How you verified correctness
- How you evaluated the result
- What tradeoffs you considered

This is a discussion of your own reasoning, not a request for a particular
implementation.

## Use of AI

The problem statement permits using AI to learn and explore ideas, but the
submitted implementation must remain your own work. Do not paste generated or
third-party solution code into the submission. You should be able to explain
and defend everything you submit during the discussion.
